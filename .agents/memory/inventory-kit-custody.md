---
name: Kit custody and recovery
description: Why kit breakdown depends on original contents and current ownership rather than catalog recipes.
---
Kit breakdown must recover the original physically recorded contents, never today's recipe. Purchased/imported kits without trustworthy assembly or explicit vendor receiving-inspection evidence must not silently acquire component provenance.

**Why:** Kit recipes can change; quantity-only stock can mix batches. Inventing lineage can create components that were never consumed, or recover the same kit twice.

**How to apply:** Preserve original assembly evidence across transfers, nesting, work use, teardown, retries, and backups. Admit older quantity-only assemblies only when their ownership is unambiguous. Separate damaged custody from usable stock; missing and scrapped identities remain unavailable. In work-order screens and printed reports, distinguish the current planned recipe from original physical contents. A partial batch must not be presented as proof of individual component serials, and configured labor allowances are not proof of actual worked time.

Purchased finished kits bring their components inside the kit, not from existing loose stock. Explicit inspection may establish their provenance, but it must never require those component serials to appear as separate purchase-receipt lines or loose balances.

**Why:** The receiving evidence concerns the finished purchased unit. Treating its internals as separately received or assembled loose stock either rejects valid vendor kits or fabricates stock movements.

**How to apply:** Validate actual scans/counts against expected contents and current serial custody. Preserve the original finished-kit receipt link, and consume only finished stock during installation.

A matching legacy serialized label alone is not continuous custody.

**Why:** A count can remove an old kit, then a later purchase/import can supply another kit with that same label. Recovering against the old assembly would create unrelated component stock.

**How to apply:** Infer legacy serial lineage only through uninterrupted, exact identity movements. A replacement receipt, uncertain count, or unsupported custody transition invalidates the inference even when the serial currently matches.

Historical membership in an assembly is not a permanent ban on reusing a component serial.

**Why:** Successful breakdown releases usable components, and a finished kit label may be reused for a later assembly with different contents.

**How to apply:** Evaluate current custody and the newest applicable assembly, not an unrestricted search of all historical assembly receipts. Preserve descendant custody when recovering an intact nested kit.
